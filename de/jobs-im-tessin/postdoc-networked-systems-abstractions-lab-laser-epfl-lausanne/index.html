Mission

The EPFL Networked Systems Abstractions Lab (LASeR) develops programming languages and formal reasoning tools for networked systems.

Networks are the invisible infrastructure that connects the modern world, yet programming and reasoning about them remains surprisingly difficult.

  • Mission
  • The EPFL Networked Systems Abstractions Lab (LASeR) develops programming languages and formal reasoning tools for networked systems.
  • Main duties and responsibilities
  • Conducting research on network abstractions, spanning language design, implementation, and verification.

Hauptaufgaben

  • Main duties and responsibilities
  • Conducting research on network abstractions, spanning language design, implementation, and verification.
  • The precise focus will be discussed with the successful candidate depending on their background, expertise and affinities. Possible directions include:
  • Design and semantics of network programming languages in the NetKAT family, including quantitative and probabilistic extensions, and scalable verification via symbolic automata and related decision procedures
  • Routing algebras: composable, algebraic representations of distributed routing protocols, as a principled basis for the compilation and verification of protocols such as BGP
  • Formal abstractions for packet scheduling: expressive specifications of scheduling policies, and compilation to programmable hardware with provable guarantees
  • AI-based cybersecurity: applying learning and AI-assisted techniques to network security, e.g., automata learning from security logs, validation of protocol models, and verified defensive controllers
  • Build a strong network in the programming languages, verification, and networking communities, including collaboration with the lab’s academic and industrial partners
  • Contribute to the design, implementation, and open-source release of the lab’s tools and artefacts
  • Participate in the supervision of PhD and master students Profile
